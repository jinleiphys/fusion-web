Elastic scattering

A projectile wave meets a nucleus that both bends it and swallows part of it. The surface shows the wave itself: behind the nucleus a shadow forms, from absorption and from interference of the bent waves.

Scope

The target is treated as spin 0 and inert: no target spin, no excitation (a warning gives its NUBASE2020 Jπ when that is not 0+); targets must be NUBASE2020 nuclides. The result is shape (direct) elastic scattering only: compound-nucleus elastic, which adds to the measured elastic cross section at low incident energies (KD02 Sec. 2.3), is not included, so at low energies a measured elastic cross section can lie above the curve for that reason alone.

Rendering

What is drawn: the surface height is Re(ψ e−iωt) (exaggerated vertically, ω not to scale) and its colour is |ψ|² on a log scale (|ψ|² = 1 is the undisturbed beam; darker blue is shadow, gold is focus). The water look (sky reflections at the Fresnel factor of water, a key-light glint, troughs tinted deeper, light through the crests) is lighting only, and the reflection is capped so the colour stays readable. The glass sphere has the radius RV of the real potential; the amber glow inside it is the absorptive potential −W(r) as solved (volume plus surface term, times W ×), drawn as emission proportional to −W along the line of sight, upper half only; at W × 0 it is empty. The basin, plinth, beam source and lights are decoration.

Potentials: n, p from Koning & Delaroche, Nucl. Phys. A 713, 231 (2003), transcribed from Koning's kd02.f; d from Han, Shi & Shen, Phys. Rev. C 74, 044615 (2006), Eqs. (1) to (13) and Table I; α from Avrigeanu, Avrigeanu & Mănăilescu, Phys. Rev. C 90, 044612 (2014), Table II. Solved live: Numerov in each (l, j), matched to Coulomb functions (Steed's method). Checked against ECIS-06 (the code KD02 was fitted with) in 14 configurations, relativistic and non-relativistic, n, p, d, α to 200 MeV: k, μ, η to 5×10−11, σR to 4×10−8, and up to 52 MeV dσ/dΩ to 5×10−5 and Ay, iT11 to 4×10−5. At 100 to 200 MeV the two differ at back angles by the step error of each: against its own finest step, this solver converges as h4 (Numerov) down to about 10−4, ECIS as h3 (shown for p and n + 208Pb at 200 MeV and d + 208Pb at 140 MeV). Against FRESCO in 14 configurations (non-relativistic, and relativistic for neutrons): σR to 1×10−6, dσ/dΩ to 5×10−5, iT11 to 3×10−5. In the nine configurations tested (n, p, d, α, 19 to 200 MeV), the step, matching radius and partial waves used here put σR within 4×10−5, dσ/dΩ within 10−3 and iT11 within 2×10−4 of converged values; other configurations are not covered by these numbers. Data: EXFOR, each table cross-checked against the IAEA's own parse.
