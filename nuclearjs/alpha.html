Alpha decay

Gamow's picture: an alpha particle rattles inside the nucleus and leaks out through the Coulomb barrier. The leak is exponentially small, so the half-life follows Qα over thirty decades.

Rendering

What is drawn: the landscape is the potential of the radial equation the drawn wave solves, −V0′ inside R and the Coulomb potential outside, each plus the centrifugal term ħ²l(l+1)/2μr² (zero for l = 0), as a solid, height in MeV and radius in fm each scaled to fit (scales differ per nucleus), warm where it exceeds Q (classically forbidden), cool where it is below Q, with 5 MeV contours and 10 fm rings. It is cut open towards you, the cut turning with the camera, so the well and the inside of the barrier stay visible; the cut faces are the same profile, hatched above Q, and the amber line across them at height Q, from R to b, is the tunnelling path (for l > 0 it also crosses the centrifugal core near the centre, classically forbidden as well, where the regular wave falls off as rl+1; that stretch is not part of the escape path). The glass sheet is the level Q. The wave rides on Q with height ∝ log10|u| over 40 decades (linear inside R); its ribbon runs just in front of the two cut faces. The well floor is drawn at the depth V0′ the drawn wave solves (see Model), not at the 35 MeV of the half-life, and for l > 0 it carries the centrifugal term inside R as well, so the drawn wave solves the drawn profile for every l; that term diverges at r = 0, so the profile is cut flat at 1.2 times its height at R, above which nothing is drawn. The alpha particle (2 p + 2 n) is not to size, and its bounce is not to time. Materials, lights, glow and the stand are decoration.

Model, exactly
Qα from AME2020 (Q(α) of the rct1 table, with its uncertainty, which carries the correlation between parent and daughter masses; Z ≥ 50). Below Z = 50, Q = B(Z−2, N−2) + B(4He) − B(Z, N) from the NUBASE2020 binding energies, whose 4-decimal B/A leave each B uncertain by up to A × 5×10−5 MeV (that bound is the error bar there). The Q error and its effect on log T are shown. Electron screening: Q is raised by (65.3 Z7/5 − 80.0 Z2/5) eV of the parent. The alpha and the daughter are a two-body system in the centre-of-mass frame with their reduced mass, so recoil is exact and needs no extra correction. Sharp surface at R = r0(Ad1/3 + 41/3), r0 = 1.20 fm. Inside, a flat well, alpha kinetic energy Q + V0, V0 = 35 MeV (25 or 50 MeV moves the even-even rms by < 0.001). Outside, V = 2Zde²/r + ħ²l(l+1)/2μr². The drawn wave: inside R the regular solution ul(K′r) = K′r jl(K′r) of the flat well with its centrifugal term, −V0′ + ħ²l(l+1)/2μr² (the profile drawn inside R), joined in value and slope to the exact outgoing Coulomb wave at R. A 35 MeV well generally has no level exactly at Q, while the formula below assumes one (for l = 0, sin²KR = K²/(K² + κR²)); the well that has it, depth V0′ (39.6 MeV for 212Po, 41.5 MeV for 238U), is the one drawn and solved. The half-life keeps V0 = 35 MeV, where it enters only through K. λ = S ν P, ν = ħK/(2μR) = vin/2R, P = [4KκR/(K² + κR²)] exp(−2∫Rbκ dr), Langer (l + ½)². This is the WKB limit of the decay width of the quasi-stationary state with the outgoing Coulomb wave G + iF; the exact form, with 1/(Fl² + Gl²) and G′/G at kR, is computed too and differs by at most 0.008 in log T over all 624 decays. S = 0.041 (log S = −1.391) is the one fitted constant, the mean of log(Tcalc/Texp) over the even-even set (187 decays, 264Hs left out, see below); rms 0.371. It is not a measured preformation probability: r0 and S are nearly degenerate (any r0 from 1.0 to 1.3 fm fits with rms 0.34 to 0.43 once S is refitted), and S absorbs the nuclear attraction that a sharp surface leaves out of the barrier. Texp = T½/BR with BR the total alpha branch of NUBASE2020, which includes decays to excited daughter states, while the calculation is ground state to ground state. The ground-state branch alone is smaller (238U: 79%), so the true partial g.s. half-life is longer than Texp: by about 0.1 decade for an even-even parent like 238U, and by more for odd ones, whose g.s. transition is often hindered. l: 0 for even-even (0+ → 0+); otherwise the lowest value allowed by the ground-state Jπ of parent and daughter (angular momentum and parity), 0 when either is unknown (flagged tentative). Where no l satisfies both (210Bi 1− → 0−, 238Am 1+ → (0+), 258Db 0− → 4+), there is no g.s. → g.s. alpha decay: these parents are left out of the comparison.
Masses, half-lives, Jπ, alpha branching: NUBASE2020, Kondev et al., Chin. Phys. C 45, 030001 (2021). Qα: AME2020, Wang et al., Chin. Phys. C 45, 030003 (2021). Coulomb functions under the barrier agree with an independent arbitrary-precision evaluation to 4×10−9.
Flagged, kept as tabulated, left out of the statistics: 264Hs (hollow point). NUBASE2020 lists T1/2 = 0.7(3) s with a 70% alpha branch, and the model gives 3.3 decades shorter; Sato et al., J. Phys. Soc. Jpn. 80, 094201 (2011) report the half-life in milliseconds (of order 0.6 ms), which the model would match to 0.3 decades. A unit slip in the table is likely but not established here. Even-even rms: 0.371 without it, 0.443 with the tabulated 0.7 s, 0.371 if it were 0.7 ms.
